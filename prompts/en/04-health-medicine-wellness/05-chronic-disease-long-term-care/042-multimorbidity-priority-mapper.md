# MULTIMORBIDITY PRIORITY MAPPER

Main objective:

> Prioritize care across multiple chronic conditions while identifying treatment conflicts and burden.

## 1. CONTEXT & LIMITS

- define the exact question and decision
- record age / population, relevant conditions and medications
- identify source date, units, method and reference standard where applicable
- separate screening, diagnosis, monitoring and prognosis
- state what cannot be concluded from the available information
- escalate urgent or dangerous patterns before routine interpretation

## 2. SPECIALIZED WORKFLOW

- list conditions and current burden
- identify competing recommendations
- rank goals by risk and patient priorities
- reduce treatment burden where possible
- surface interaction conflicts
- coordinate responsible clinicians

## 3. EVIDENCE STANDARD

Use current high-quality guidelines, systematic reviews, authoritative laboratory / professional standards and primary evidence appropriate to the question. Always distinguish population evidence from an individualized clinical conclusion.

## 4. DOMAIN MODEL

Treat chronic care as a longitudinal system: goals, treatment, monitoring, complications, self-management, coordination and escalation must remain aligned over time.

## 5. SAFETY / RED-FLAG TEST

- acute deterioration overrides routine chronic-care planning
- new neurologic, respiratory, cardiac, severe infection or major bleeding signs require urgent assessment
- monitoring trends must be interpreted with baseline and treatment changes
- multimorbidity can make single-disease guidelines conflict

## 8. REQUIRED OUTPUT

1. Executive summary.
2. Context and assumptions.
3. Evidence-based analysis.
4. Key findings and limitations.
5. Required matrices.
6. Safety / escalation points.
7. What requires clinical confirmation.
8. Calibrated conclusion without false certainty.

End with **Medical Integrity Check** confirming that evidence, units, context, uncertainty and safety boundaries are explicit.

This supports chronic-care preparation and does not replace individualized care from a qualified clinician.

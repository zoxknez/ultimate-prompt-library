# FINANCIAL RISK AUDIT

I want a deep, evidence-first and risk-oriented analysis that clearly separates threat, vulnerability, control weakness, inherent risk, residual risk and scenario hardening.

Main objective:

> Assess material financial risks across liquidity, credit, market, concentration, funding, covenant, counterparty and cash-flow exposures, including how they interact under stress.

This is not:
- a generic risk checklist
- listing hypothetical risks without exposure
- assuming every control is inadequate
- mixing inherent and residual risk
- risk scoring without transparent methodology
- a compliance claim without a defined requirement and evidence

## 1. CONTEXT DISCOVERY

Establish:
- business model, critical processes and objectives
- regulatory and contractual context
- risk appetite, tolerance and limits where defined
- critical assets, data, people, suppliers and platforms
- financial and operational dependencies
- incident/loss history and near misses
- existing controls and control owners
- recovery capability and contingency options
- decision context and time horizon

## 2. EVIDENCE MODEL

A - direct incident, loss, control test, contract, audit, transaction, telemetry or regulatory evidence  
B - complete risk-control evidence chain or multiple independent reconciled sources  
C - strong derived analysis with transparent assumptions and model  
D - credible inference requiring verification  
E - scenario, emerging risk or hardening recommendation

Status: CONFIRMED / SUPPORTED / NOT VERIFIED / CONTESTED / NOT APPLICABLE / SCENARIO

## 26. CONTROL EFFECTIVENESS TEST

For every important control test design, implementation, operating effectiveness, frequency, owner, evidence artifact, failure mode and compensating control. Separate control exists from control works.

## 27. FALSE-POSITIVE PROTECTION

Do not report a risk as material merely because a scenario is possible. Require a real exposure path, relevant consequence and context-appropriate likelihood or trigger. Separate risk hardening from confirmed gaps.

## 28. SEVERITY

P0 - near-term existential, safety, fraud, compliance shutdown or liquidity failure path  
P1 - material risk/control failure with major financial, operational or regulatory impact  
P2 - significant residual risk above tolerance or serious control gap  
P3 - limited weakness, monitoring gap or insufficient evidence  
P4 - hardening, scenario or future resilience improvement

## 31. FAILURE CHAINS

Model at least 5 concrete risk chains:

```text
condition
↓
trigger
↓
control failure or exposure
↓
operational / financial / compliance event
↓
business consequence
```

At least one scenario must combine two or more risks and show correlation/contagion effects.

## 32. SCENARIO AND STRESS TEST

Test when relevant:
- loss of a key customer or counterparty
- supplier/platform outage
- fraud or control override
- liquidity squeeze and slower collections
- regulatory incident
- simultaneous operational and financial shock

## 33. ADVERSARIAL SECOND PASS

- look for risks understated by current scorecards
- test control failure, not only control presence
- look for concentration and common-mode failure
- verify recovery time from real exercises
- test management override
- look for accepted risk without an explicit owner
- check whether the KRI actually precedes the loss event

## 34. FINAL QUALITY GATE

Confirm risk statement, inherent/residual distinction, control effectiveness, owner, KRI, risk appetite/tolerance where defined, treatment, contingency, combined scenario, false-positive protection and verification steps.

## 35. OUTPUT

`FINANCIAL_RISK_AUDIT.md`

The report begins with Executive summary, Risk posture, Top residual risks, Control weaknesses, KRIs, Findings, Risk/Control matrices, Failure chains, Scenario stress test, Treatment plan and Escalation triggers.

# FINAL RULE

A good risk audit does not create the longest possible risk list. It shows which exposure can actually change the business outcome, which controls truly work, what remains as residual risk and when the organization must act.
